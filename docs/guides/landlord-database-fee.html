The landlord database fee: £65 a year, and what you pay first

Written by GenZAgents, reviewed 3 November 2026. Sources below.

The fee to register on England's landlord database is £65 per property per year, but your first payment is smaller. Nobody is charged for days before 15 November 2027, so a landlord who registers on 15 December 2026 pays £5.34 a property, and the most anyone pays the first time during the rollout is £64.82. Your renewal date is one year after you register your first property, and every property you add later takes the same date. All of this is in the official guidance for landlords published on 29 October 2026, including the formula the service uses.

See your first payment

What the fee covers

The fee is for each property entry on the "Register your rental property" service, and it runs for a year. It is per property, not per landlord. The official guidance says the fees also fund council enforcement work.

£65 times the number of properties, from your first renewal date. The fee is not subject to VAT and is tax-deductible (official guidance). Your first payment is smaller (below).

Renewal: one date for the portfolio

Under the draft regulations (regulation 10), the renewal date is the end of the 12 months that start on the day your entry was first made, and a second or later property takes the same renewal date. The official guidance (29 October 2026) now says the same: "all properties that you register will have the same renewal date". An entry that is not renewed becomes inactive.

For example, if your first property is registered on 15 December 2026, the guidance gives 15 December 2027 as the renewal date. A property you add in July 2027 renews on that same day, and pays only for the days up to it.

What you actually pay the first time

The official guidance (29 October 2026) sets out how the service pro-rates the fee. Nobody is charged for any day before 15 November 2027, the date by which every landlord in England has to be registered. Your first payment covers only the days from 15 November 2027 to your renewal date, and your renewal date is one year after you register your first property. After that you pay the full £65 a property each year.

So a landlord who registers on the first day, 15 Dec 2026, pays £5.34 a property, then £65 on 15 Dec 2027. The latest you can register in the rollout is the South West deadline, and even then the first payment is under £65.

Already licensed by your council? You pay both

If your council runs selective licensing, or additional licensing for shared houses, a licence does not count as registration. You need the licence and the database entry, and you pay both fees. The draft regulations have no exemption or discount for licensed homes. The only change they make to who is covered is to take out supported exempt accommodation (regulation 3), and they leave the fee to the database operator (regulation 14). When you register, the service asks whether the property needs an HMO, additional or selective licence and for the licence number (draft Schedule 3, paragraph 10). The NRLA has asked the government to explain how the two systems will fit together so that landlords do not pay twice. Nothing has changed yet.

On 28 October 2026 we read the website of each of England's 296 local housing authorities. 72 councils have a selective or additional licensing scheme in force or about to start: 53 with selective licensing and 51 with additional HMO licensing. Most selective schemes cover only some wards. When you run the free check, rentready shows each property's council and its schemes, with a link to the council's page. The full list of council schemes has a postcode check and shows which schemes end in the next year. Each region guide lists every council (for example West Midlands or London).

Budgeting for it

Allow a small first payment (see the table above), then £65 per property per year from your renewal date. With the certificates you upload, the real first-year bill is about £125 to £555 a property (first-year cost and calculator). If you hold properties in several regions, you can register them all from 15 December 2026. You do not have to wait for each region's start date.

Skipping the fee is not a saving. Penalties are up to £7,000 for a breach (section 91) and up to £40,000 for the offences in section 92: false information, continuing a breach after a penalty, or a repeat within five years. Without an active entry, the court cannot make a possession order, except on Ground 7A or Ground 14 (section 90). See the penalties guide.

Questions

How much is the landlord database fee?

£65 per property per year, but nobody pays for days before 15 November 2027. Your first payment covers only the days from then to your renewal date: £5.34 a property if you register on 15 December 2026, and never more than £64.82 during the rollout.

When do I renew?

Every 12 months from your first entry, and properties added later take the same renewal date. This is in the draft regulations and in the official guidance published on 29 October 2026.

What if I add a property part way through the year?

It pays for the days from 15 November 2027 (or the day you add it, if later) up to your shared renewal date, then renews with the rest at £65. A property added on 1 March 2027 to a portfolio first registered on 15 December 2026 pays £5.34.

I already have a council licence. Do I still pay the £65?

Yes. A selective or additional licence does not replace registration, and the draft regulations give no exemption or discount for licensed homes. You need both and pay both fees, and the service asks for your licence number. 72 of England's 296 councils had a selective or additional scheme when we checked on 28 October 2026.

Is the fee per landlord or per property?

Per property. Each property you register has its own £65 annual fee.
