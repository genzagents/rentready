HMRC signed me up for Making Tax Digital: what landlords do now

Written by GenZAgents, reviewed 1 November 2026. Sources below.

If HMRC has written to say it signed you up for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, its records show more than £50,000 of property and self-employment income on your 2024-25 return. You now need software, digital records back to 6 April 2026, and the overdue quarterly update. The next update is due on 7 November 2026 and covers 6 April to 5 October, so it also makes up a missed August update. There are no penalty points for late updates this year. HMRC's figure can be wrong, though, so check it first.

The five steps after HMRC signs you up

  1. Sign in to HMRC online services with the details you use for Self Assessment and choose Making Tax Digital for Income Tax. You may find the confirmation letter there, or it may come by post.
  2. Check the income sources HMRC holds. They come from your 2024-25 return (and 2025-26 if you had already filed it). All your UK lets count as one UK property business, and all overseas lets as one foreign property business. Add anything new and tell HMRC about anything that has stopped.
  3. Choose compatible software. HMRC does not provide any. Check the product covers UK property, your update periods and the tax return, then authorise it. Filter HMRC's list to products that do the whole job.
  4. Catch up. Create digital records of rent and expenses from 6 April 2026, then send the overdue quarterly update as soon as you can. Your HMRC account lists what is overdue and what is next. Work out the figures from your bank statement.
  5. Keep going. One update a quarter (7 February and 7 May 2027 are next), then the 2026-27 tax return from the software by 31 January 2028.

Each update is a running total for the year so far, not a tax return. HMRC will not give penalty points for a late update in 2026-27. From 2027-28 every late update earns a point, and four points means a £200 penalty. The tax return deadline still carries points this year.

Automatic ones include income of £20,000 or less, no National Insurance number before the tax year, a power of attorney or deputy recorded on the 2024-25 return, and for 2026-27 only: foster or kinship carers claiming qualifying care relief, averaging relief as a farmer or creative, and returns with the SA107 or SA109 pages.

Partnerships are outside MTD for now; partnership rental income is reported on the partnership return.

Questions

Why has HMRC signed me up for Making Tax Digital?

Because its records show your qualifying income was over £50,000 in the 2024-25 tax year and you had not signed up yourself or through an agent. Since September 2026 HMRC has been signing these people up in stages and then writing to them, in HMRC online services or by post. Qualifying income is gross property income (your share of a jointly owned property) plus self-employment turnover, before expenses.

What do I do after HMRC signs me up for MTD?

Sign in to HMRC online services with your Self Assessment details, select Making Tax Digital for Income Tax and confirm the income sources HMRC holds (all UK lets count as one UK property business). Then choose compatible software, create digital records back to 6 April 2026 and send the overdue quarterly update as soon as you can. The 7 November 2026 update covers 6 April to 5 October, so it also makes up a missed August update.

Will I get a penalty for missing quarterly updates after being signed up?

No penalty points for late quarterly updates in the 2026-27 tax year. You still have to send the final update before you can file the 2026-27 tax return, and penalty points do apply if that return is late (due 31 January 2028).

What if HMRC signed me up by mistake?

If all your property and self-employment income stopped before 6 April 2026, update your details in your HMRC online account; you will not need MTD but must still file your 2025-26 return by 31 January 2027. If you think your qualifying income was £50,000 or less, or an exemption applies, contact Self Assessment general enquiries. If you are digitally excluded you can apply for an exemption.
