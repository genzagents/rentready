Do I need to register every rental property on the landlord database?

Written by GenZAgents, reviewed 31 October 2026. Sources below.

Yes: every rental home in England let on an assured or regulated tenancy needs its own entry on the landlord database (the PRS Database, "Register your rental property"), at £65 a property a year, plus one entry for you as the landlord. The test is the kind of tenancy, not the kind of building. Holiday lets, company lets, lodgers in your own home, social housing, supported exempt accommodation and some student, very high and very low rent lets are not assured tenancies, so they are outside the database. Empty properties do not need registering yet.

One entry per home, not per building or per landlord

The Act's unit is the dwelling: "a building or part of a building which is occupied or intended to be occupied as a separate dwelling" (section 63). So a house converted into three self-contained flats is three entries and £195 a year, and a landlord with ten flats in one block pays for ten. You also make one landlord entry for yourself, which has no fee of its own: the draft regulations charge only for dwelling entries (regulations 6(5) and 14); the £65 is per dwelling (fee guide).

Joint owners do not each register the same home. The draft regulations allow only one active entry for a dwelling at a time (regulation 6(6)). The one exception is a sale with tenants in place: after exchange, the buyer can make a second entry so the home is never unregistered (regulation 6(7)). Each joint landlord still gets their own landlord identifier alongside the single dwelling identifier (MHCLG's answers to the TLIC committee, 28 October 2026).

Shared houses (HMOs): the dwelling entry asks for the number of occupants, the number of households and the HMO licence number (Schedule 3), which reads as one entry for the shared house rather than one per room. The official guidance has not said so in terms yet; we will update this page when it does.

Which lets go on the database

The duty falls on a "residential landlord": the landlord under an assured tenancy (Housing Act 1988) or a regulated tenancy (Rent Act 1977) of a dwelling in England that is not social housing (section 63). An assured tenancy needs a tenant who is an individual living there as their only or principal home (Housing Act 1988, section 1), and Schedule 1 to that Act lists tenancies that can never be assured.

Empty properties and adverts

For now you register properties that are let, or that become let during the rollout. The official guidance says empty properties do not need registering yet. That changes later: the Act already says you must not market a home for a residential tenancy without active landlord and dwelling entries, and every written advert must show both unique identifiers (section 82). MHCLG says that part will not start before the rollout ends on 15 November 2027, and that adverts will then need the identifiers of every joint landlord as well as the dwelling (MHCLG's answers to the TLIC committee). Between tenancies you will need an active entry while you market the home; if you are not marketing it, you can deactivate the entry. You can already make an entry for a home you intend to let, with fewer details (draft regulation 6(3)).

What could still change

The Act lets the government widen or narrow the database by regulations: adding or removing kinds of tenancy or licence, including superior landlords, or counting structures and vehicles as dwellings (section 63(4)). The draft regulations use that power once, to take out supported exempt accommodation, and both Houses of Parliament approved them on 4 November 2026. We re-check the legislation and the official guidance each week.

Questions

Do I need to register every rental property on the PRS Database?

Every home in England you let on an assured or regulated tenancy needs its own dwelling entry and £65 fee, plus one landlord entry for you. Holiday lets, company lets, lodgers, social housing and supported exempt accommodation are outside it.

Do I need to register an empty property?

Not yet. The official guidance says to register properties that are let or become let during the rollout. Later, you will have to register before you advertise a property, and adverts must show both registration numbers.

Do company lets and holiday lets need registering?

No. A company let is not an assured tenancy because the tenant must be an individual, and lettings for a holiday cannot be assured tenancies, so neither is on the database unless the government changes the rules by regulations.
