Letting agents, portfolios and the landlord database

A letting agent cannot complete the registration for a landlord. The official guidance says landlords must start and end the registration themselves. An agent or property manager can upload certain information for the landlord, as agreed between them. The landlord stays responsible for providing all the required information.

What an agent can and cannot do

  • The landlord starts the entry, with GOV.UK One Login, and finishes it.
  • An agent or property manager can upload certain information, if the landlord agrees.
  • The landlord remains responsible for everything the service asks for.

The official guidance says guidance for agents is due before the service launches. Agents should check gov.uk for it.

Who can register for a landlord

Some people can make the entry for a landlord in a legal capacity. The official guidance lists, for example, a donee of a power of attorney, a personal representative, a court-appointed deputy, a guardian, a receiver, a liquidator, an administrator or a mortgagee. They need a certified or sealed copy of their authority. A letting agent acting under a management agreement is not on that list.

Marketing and adverts

Section 82 of the Renters' Rights Act 2025 says a person must not market a dwelling for a residential tenancy without active landlord and dwelling entries. Any written advert must include the landlord's and the dwelling's unique identifiers (section 82). These rules apply to whoever markets or advertises the property, not only the landlord. Gov.uk says letting agents and landlords will need to include registration numbers on adverts (gov.uk news).

Today the duty to register covers let properties. The official guidance says future legislation will require unoccupied properties to be registered before you or your agent market them.

Registering a portfolio

A landlord makes one entry for themselves, then one for each property. The rules for a portfolio:

  • The fee is £65 per property per year. Ten properties cost £650 a year.
  • Properties added later take the same renewal date as your existing entries, 12 months from the first (in the draft regulations, regulation 10, and reported by the NRLA; not yet in official guidance). Later properties pay pro rata for their first year (the official guidance says the fee is pro-rated during the rollout; how it works for later properties is reported by the NRLA).
  • Each property works to its own region's deadline, set by where the property is.
  • Registration opens for all of England on 15 December 2026, and you can register properties in several regions at once.

Each property needs its own uploads: the gas safety record if it has gas, the EICR and the EPC. With many properties, the slow part is finding certificates that expire before the deadline and booking renewals in time.

Questions

Can a letting agent register a property for a landlord?

No. The official guidance says landlords must start and end the registration themselves. An agent can upload certain information if the landlord agrees.

Who is responsible if information is missing?

The landlord. The official guidance says the landlord remains responsible for providing all required information.

Do adverts need database numbers?

Section 82 of the Renters' Rights Act 2025 says written adverts for a residential tenancy must include the landlord's and the dwelling's unique identifiers.

How does renewal work for a portfolio?

Properties added later take the same renewal date as your existing entries, under the draft regulations. The official guidance says the fee is pro-rated during the rollout; the NRLA reports that later properties pay pro rata to the renewal date. The single renewal date is not in official guidance yet.
