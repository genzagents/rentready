What ministers told Parliament about the landlord database

Written by GenZAgents, reviewed 4 November 2026, from the Hansard record of both debates on the draft regulations. Sources below.

There will be no bulk upload: every rented home needs its own entry, the landlord (not the agent) must start each one, and each is paid for separately unless the minister's promised letter says otherwise. That is what the Housing Minister, Matthew Pennycook, told a Commons committee and Baroness Taylor of Stevenage told the Lords on 28 October 2026, when MPs and peers pressed them on the questions below. The good news: you do not re-enter everything each year, and HMO rooms do not need separate entries.

The questions, and the answers on the record

  1. Can I bulk upload my properties?

    No. Pennycook said the government looked at bulk uploads, including Excel files, and decided against them because of data validation and error handling problems; asked to commit to bulk upload, he said he could not. Organisations will be able to add several employees to one account, and the government is "looking at" a secure connection that property management software could use instead. No date was given for it.

    For you: Plan to enter every home by hand. Gather each property's details and certificate dates in one place before your window opens, so each entry is copy and paste rather than a hunt through files.

    Source: Commons committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Matthew Pennycook) · Lords Grand Committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Baroness Taylor)

  2. Does each flat in a block need its own entry? What about HMOs?

    Each dwelling is registered separately, even when one landlord owns a whole building of rented flats, because rent, occupants and gas and electrical certificates differ from flat to flat. Baroness Taylor added that in an HMO the individual rooms do not need to be registered.

    For you: Count homes, not buildings and not rooms: a block of 12 flats is 12 entries and 12 fees; a five-bedroom HMO is one.

    Source: Lords Grand Committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Baroness Taylor)

  3. Can I pay for all my properties in one go?

    Not confirmed. Lord Fuller said each property is paid for by a separate card transaction. Baroness Taylor said her understanding was that one payment could cover several, called the point fair, and promised to reply in writing.

    For you: Assume one card payment per property until the service or the minister's letter says otherwise, and check your card's daily limit if you have many homes.

    Source: Lords Grand Committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Baroness Taylor)

  4. Can my letting agent register my properties for me?

    No. Both ministers said the landlord must start each registration, so that landlords engage with their duties, and stays responsible for registration, payment, renewal and accuracy. Once you have started, you can let an agent who agrees add the tenancy and health and safety details. Agents have no mandatory role and are not registered themselves.

    For you: Start the entry yourself, then hand the property link to your agent for the certificates and tenancy details. You are fined if it is wrong, so check what they enter.

    Source: Commons committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Matthew Pennycook) · Lords Grand Committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Baroness Taylor)

  5. Will I have to enter everything again every year?

    No. Pennycook said landlords update only what has changed; the annual renewal is a prompt to confirm the entry is still right, like a register of interests. The regulations say out-of-date details must generally be corrected within 28 days.

    For you: Keep a note of each certificate's expiry: a new gas safety record or EICR is a change you must add within 28 days, renewal or not.

    Source: Commons committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Matthew Pennycook) · Lords Grand Committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Baroness Taylor)

  6. I already have a council licence. Do I register twice?

    Yes, for now. Both ministers said the database will not replace or duplicate selective licensing because they have different purposes, and that the government will keep reviewing how the two interact, with a view to reducing duplication.

    For you: In a licensing area you pay the licence fee and £65 a property a year. See which councils license.

    Source: Commons committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Matthew Pennycook) · Lords Grand Committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Baroness Taylor)

  7. Why is the fee £65 when it was meant to be about £20?

    Baroness Taylor said £65 a year a property is needed to run the database and fund enforcement, part of the fee goes to councils for enforcement, the operator sets it rather than the regulations, and it is not meant to pay for costs outside private renting. No breakdown was given. Peers quoted the earlier impact assessment figure of £28.58 every three years.

    For you: Budget £65 a property a year from 15 December 2027, with a small pro rata first payment before that. Work out your first payment.

    Source: Lords Grand Committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Baroness Taylor) · Commons committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Matthew Pennycook)

  8. How do I log in, and is it secure?

    Organisational landlords log in with GOV.UK One Login. The minister said the service is being assessed against the National Cyber Security Centre's framework and will be penetration tested. Peers objected that agents get a per-property web link that does not change from year to year.

    For you: Treat each property link you send an agent like a password: send it only to the person who needs it.

    Source: Lords Grand Committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Baroness Taylor)

  9. Has it been tested?

    Baroness Taylor said user research with landlords began in 2023 and formal testing in May 2026: almost 90% of organisational landlords tested found the service easy or very easy, and 80% found the guidance clear. Peers asked who was tested and whether anyone tried a large portfolio; she promised more detail.

    For you: Expect it to work for a single property and to be slow for a large portfolio.

    Source: Lords Grand Committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Baroness Taylor)

  10. How will landlords who never register be caught?

    Once a later set of regulations makes part of the database public, tenants will be able to check a property and report one that is not registered to the council. That public side is a second instrument, due after the rollout ends.

    For you: Councils see every entry from day one. See what the public will see, and when.

    Source: Lords Grand Committee, 28 Oct 2026 (Baroness Taylor)

How long will your portfolio take?

The government assumed about 10 minutes a property; Lord Fuller told the Lords that letting professionals had not yet finished one in under 20. Enter how many homes you let (count each flat, not each HMO room).

Where the regulations are now

The Commons committee approved the draft regulations on 28 October by 11 votes to 6 and the Lords Grand Committee considered them the same day. The full House of Lords approved them on 4 November 2026; the Commons decision was deferred to a vote on 4 November. The live status page shows the result as soon as Parliament publishes it. Ministers said a second set of regulations, after the rollout, will decide what the public can see.

Questions

Can landlords bulk upload properties to the PRS database?

No. The Housing Minister told the Commons committee on 28 October 2026 that bulk upload, including Excel files, was ruled out over data validation and error handling, and he would not commit to it. Each dwelling needs its own entry. Organisations can add several employees to one account, and the government is looking at a secure connection for property management software, with no date.

Can a letting agent register a property on the landlord database?

No. The landlord must start each registration and remains responsible for it. After that the landlord can delegate the tenancy and health and safety details to an agent who agrees.

Do HMO rooms need to be registered separately on the landlord database?

No. The minister told the Lords Grand Committee that individual rooms in an HMO do not need to be registered. Each separate dwelling, such as each flat in a block, does.

Do landlords re-enter their details every year?

No. Only changed details need updating; the annual renewal confirms the entry is still correct. Out-of-date details must generally be corrected within 28 days.
