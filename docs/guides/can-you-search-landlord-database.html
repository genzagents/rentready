Can you search the landlord database?

Written by GenZAgents, reviewed 4 November 2026, from the Renters' Rights Act 2025, the draft regulations and the official landlord guidance. Sources below.

No. When England's private rented sector database (the "Register your rental property" service) opens on 15 December 2026, nothing on it is public: landlord and property details are seen only by the landlord, councils and enforcement bodies. The Renters' Rights Act 2025 lets ministers decide later, by new regulations, which details the public can see. The draft 2026 regulations make none, so there is no public search, and no date for one.

What becomes visible, and when

  1. Now, and from 15 December 2026

    Nothing on the database is public. Your landlord and property entries are seen by you, the database operator, councils and other enforcement bodies.

    Who sees it: Councils, trading standards, the Greater London Authority, mayoral combined authorities, the government. Source: gov.uk guidance; Act s.86(2)

  2. Not before 15 November 2027

    Every written advert for a let must show the landlord's and the property's unique identifiers, and a home cannot be marketed without active entries. This is the first thing tenants will see.

    Who sees it: Anyone reading the advert. Source: Act s.82 and s.84; MHCLG to TLIC

  3. Later, by new regulations

    Some details from landlord and property entries become public. Which fields, and how they are shown, is left to regulations not yet drafted.

    Who sees it: The public. Source: Act s.86(1)(a) and (d)

  4. Later, by new regulations

    Banning orders, convictions and council fines for landlord offences, entered by councils and linked to your landlord entry, published no sooner than 21 days after you are told, so you can point out errors.

    Who sees it: The public. Source: Act s.83 and s.86(1)(b), (c)

The Act names who must have access from the start (section 86(2)): councils, local trading standards, the lead enforcement authority, mayoral combined authorities and the Greater London Authority. Councils can also verify and correct entries (draft regulation 13). The public is not on that list; public access exists only once ministers make regulations under section 86(1).

Landlords: what this means for you

  • Register on time anyway. Not being public does not mean not checked: councils see every entry and every gap from the first day, and can fine you for a missing entry. See the penalties and your region's deadline.
  • Write each entry as if it may be read. Which fields become public is not decided. The official guidance says the privacy notice will be updated before any change to how data is shared, and registered landlords will be told. The service asks for a correspondence address in England or Wales for each property; choose one you are content to use for tenant and council letters.
  • Adverts will carry your numbers. Once section 82 starts (MHCLG says not before the rollout ends on 15 November 2027), every written advert must show your landlord identifier and the property's identifier, and you cannot market a home without active entries. Keep both numbers with your letting paperwork.
  • Fines and bans may become public. Councils must enter banning orders and the offences and fines that lead to them (section 83). If those are published, you get at least 21 days' notice first to point out mistakes.

Tenants: what you can check today

There is no public look-up on the database yet. Two public registers exist now: every council must keep a register of the licences it grants that anyone can inspect (Housing Act 2004, section 232), and London has the Mayor's Rogue Landlord Checker. Enter the property's postcode to see which apply.

Questions

Can you search the PRS database?

Not yet. When the Register your rental property service opens on 15 December 2026, government guidance says landlord and property information will not be shared publicly. The Renters' Rights Act 2025 lets ministers choose later, by regulations, which details become public (section 86), and the draft 2026 regulations do not do that. Councils and enforcement bodies can see the database from the start.

How can a tenant check if a landlord is registered?

There is no public look-up at launch. The first visible sign will be the landlord and property identifiers that every written advert must show once section 82 of the Act starts, which MHCLG says will not be before 15 November 2027. Today a tenant can check the council's public register of licences if the home is in a licensing area or is a licensed HMO, and in London the Mayor's Rogue Landlord Checker.

Will tenants see my address on the landlord database?

Nothing is public at launch, and no regulation yet says which fields will be. The Act allows ministers to publish some details later. Government guidance says the privacy notice will be updated before any change to how data is shared, and registered landlords will be told.

Will fines and banning orders be shown on the landlord database?

Councils must add an entry for a landlord under a banning order, or convicted or fined by them for a banning order offence, once appeals are over (section 83). Ministers can make those entries public and link them to the landlord's own entry, but only from at least 21 days after the council tells the landlord, so errors can be corrected (section 86).
