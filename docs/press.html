Press: rental EPCs and the landlord database

About 243,000 rental EPCs in England reach their ten-year expiry between 1 November 2026 and their region's deadline to register on the new landlord database (the PRS Database). Everything on this page is free to use with credit to rentready (GenZAgents).

Figures from the government's EPC lodgement table, data to 30 September 2026. Reviewed 31 October 2026.

243,000rental EPCs reach ten years before their region's deadline
76,000of them inside the region's own three-month window

71,500in London, the most of any region
1,640,000reach ten years in 2028 to 2030, before the planned C minimum
625,000in 2029 alone, against 305,000 in 2026

What it means for a landlord

A lapsed EPC does not stop a landlord registering an existing tenancy, but every new let needs a valid one, and the database asks for the certificate. Landlords who will re-let next year should check their dates now. The free rentready check shows, per property, the region's deadline and which certificate lapses first.

Method in brief

An EPC is valid for ten years. We take the government's quarterly count of EPCs lodged for private and social rented homes (live table D4(b)) and move each quarter forward ten years. A quarter that straddles a cut-off date is split by days. The counts are certificates, not homes: a home re-assessed within ten years is counted twice, and some landlords renew early. Full method and caveats are in the data story.
