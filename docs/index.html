What the landlord database is

From 15 December 2026 every private landlord in England registers each let property on Register your rental property (Renters' Rights Act), region by region, at £65 a property a year, uploading the gas safety record, EICR and EPC. Unoccupied properties need not be registered yet, and supported exempt accommodation is excluded (official guidance). No active entry, no possession order, except on Ground 7A or Ground 14 (section 90). Penalties up to £7,000 for a breach (section 91) and up to £40,000 for the offences in section 92: false information, continuing a breach after a penalty, or a repeat within five years.

Sources and legal status

Source: the draft Private Rented Sector Database Regulations 2026, laid before Parliament on 9 September 2026 and approved by both Houses on 4 November 2026. The House of Lords Secondary Legislation Scrutiny Committee drew them to the special attention of the House (13th report, 22 October 2026), and a Commons Delegated Legislation Committee and the Lords Grand Committee debated them on 28 October 2026; both Houses approved them on 4 November 2026, the Commons by 316 votes to 149 (where they stand now; Parliament record, checked 10 November 2026), as summarised by the NRLA and HCR Law; the same dates are in the official guidance at housinghub.campaign.gov.uk, which names the region "Yorkshire and Humber". Upload within 28 days of the entry and one renewal date for every property are in the draft regulations (regulation 6(2), regulation 10) and reported by the NRLA; the official guidance published on 29 October 2026 confirms both and gives the fee formula: no charge for days before 15 November 2027, so registering on 15 December 2026 costs £5.34 a property the first time (every region). The £65 fee is from the official guidance: the draft regulations leave the amount to the database operator (regulation 14). If the tenancy and safety documents are not uploaded within 28 days, the entry becomes inactive (regulation 11(2)(a)), and any later change must be updated within 28 days (regulation 9(4)). Check gov.uk before relying on a date. Not legal advice.
